What's on the ballot

Who you're voting for, and why it matters

A midterm has no presidential race, but many other offices are up. Here is what each one does.

U.S. House · 2-year term

Representative

Every House seat is on the ballot. Members write federal laws and approve the federal budget.

Your district's representative votes on the federal rules that affect you most.

U.S. Senate · 6-year term

Senator

Each state has two senators. About a third of the seats are up in any given election.

Senators confirm federal judges and approve treaties and many top appointments.

State executive

Governor

The governor leads the state government, proposes the state budget, and signs or vetoes state laws.

Governors shape state decisions on schools, roads, health care, and more.

State legislature

State lawmakers

State legislators write state laws, pass the state budget, and oversee state agencies.

Many rules on tuition, rent, and jobs come from state law.

Local

Local offices

This can include mayors, city council members, school boards, county officials, and some judges.

Local offices often make decisions that affect your daily life most directly.

Direct vote

Ballot measures

Some states and cities let voters decide questions directly, such as proposed laws or changes to the constitution.

Measures can pass or fail based on votes like yours, without a legislator in between.

Your sample ballot shows exactly which of these are on your ballot, since not every office is up in every area.

Ways to vote

Three ways to cast your ballot

Your state decides which options you have and the dates for each. Confirm with your election office.

By mail

Some states mail a ballot to every registered voter. Others require you to request one first. Many let you track your ballot online.

Early, in person

Many states offer in-person voting before Election Day, often at several sites. Hours and locations vary, and the lines are usually shorter.

Election Day

Polls are open for set hours. Your polling place is listed on your sample ballot or your state's lookup tool.

Glossary

Words you'll see on election sites

Sample ballot

A preview of the races and candidates for your address, so you can research before you vote.

Early voting

In-person voting that happens before Election Day, at set locations and hours.

Provisional ballot

A ballot cast when someone's eligibility is in question. It is counted only after officials verify eligibility.

Ballot curing

Fixing a mail ballot with a problem, such as a missing signature, before a deadline set by the state.

Canvass

The official review and count of votes after an election, done before results are certified.

Certification

The official confirmation of election results by the responsible officials.

This guide is nonpartisan. It does not recommend candidates or parties. Rules vary by state and county, so confirm dates and requirements with your official election office before you act.
